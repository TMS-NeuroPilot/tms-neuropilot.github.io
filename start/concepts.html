Key concepts#

Every other page assumes the vocabulary on this one. It is short on purpose: five quantities, one loop.

The five quantities#

Quantity What it is Where you meet it
TargetA coil pose the protocol wants stimulated: a position and an orientation, in SimNIBS head space.search_positions.hdf5 (the placements library), the target-order file
PulseOne stimulation at one target. A target is usually repeated n_repetitions times.the Control page, the results CSV
E-fieldThe electric field that pulse delivered to each element of the cortical region of interest, in V/m.e.hdf5 (precomputed) or the online solver
MEPThe peak-to-peak amplitude of the EMG response that pulse evoked, per channel, in mV.the MEP results page, the results CSV
R²How well the MEP amplitudes are explained by the E-field, per cortical element and per channel.the Cortical results page, the exported _roi_r2.csv

The main output is the last row: a map of R² over the cortex says where the stimulation effect is best explained by the field the coil delivered. It is a data-driven localisation of the stimulated site, not a significance map.

The loop#

  1. Choose the next target — the next entry of the target-order file, or a new marker requested from the neuronavigation.
  2. Move the coil — the robot receives the target pose, plans, moves and presses the coil against the head.
  3. Wait for the target — the neuronavigation reports the realised coil pose and whether it is within tolerance (Coil at target).
  4. Fire — the stimulator delivers one pulse at the configured amplitude.
  5. Collect — the EMG is recorded and the MEP is extracted from the window around the pulse.
  6. Associate the field — the E-field of that coil pose is taken from the precomputed library or solved online.
  7. Analyse — the pulse is appended to the regression; R² is recomputed and the cortical map is redrawn.

The loop repeats until the target list is exhausted or the operator presses Finish. The mapping loop documents the state machine in detail; E-field modes covers step 6.

Why the E-field is per pulse#

A naive mapping assumes every pulse delivers the same field to the same spot. In practice the coil sits a few millimetres away from the planned pose, the head moves, and the handle angle changes. The field therefore differs between pulses, and a regression against a nominal field blurs the map.

TMS NeuroPilot records the field of the realised pose for every pulse. Each row of the regression is (E-field vector, MEP amplitude) for one pulse, so a pulse that landed 3 mm off contributes the field it actually delivered. This is also why the E-field mode can be switched mid-run without corrupting anything: the mode decides how the row is produced, not what the row means.

The regression#

After at least three pulses, the analysis fits, for each EMG channel and each ROI element:

text
MEP_amplitude  ~  f(E_field at that element)

The coefficient of determination of that fit is the R² you see on the mesh. One fit per element is what makes the map: elements where the field varies with the response in a consistent way get a high R²; elements where it does not get a low one.

The shape of f is selectable per study (nine options, see Analysis and fits). The default is a linear fit; a sigmoid is closer to the physiological recruitment curve but needs more pulses to constrain four parameters.

ℹR² is not a significance test

A high R² says the field explains the variance in this sample. It does not say the effect is significant, nor that the element is the source of the MEP. Treat the map as a localisation aid and report it with the pulse count, the fit function and the E-field mode.

What the modes change#

The quantities above are identical in all run modes. Only who provides the peers changes:

Mode Neuronavigation Robot Stimulator EMG
simvirtual (sim_nav)virtual MuJoCo (sim_robot)virtualvirtual
roboInVesaliusyour robot via tms-robot-controlyour stimulator via magicpyyour EMG PC's share
manualInVesaliusnone: the operator moves the coilyour stimulatoryour EMG PC
trainingInVesaliusnone: the operator moves the coilyour stimulatorvirtual, driven by the E-field

Run modes compares them in full.

What TMS NeuroPilot is not#

  • It is not a medical device. It is research software; every real session needs ethics approval and local MR-safety rules.
  • It is not a neuronavigation system. It asks InVesalius to create markers and reports realised coil poses; it does not track the head itself.
  • It is not a stimulator driver. It sends fire commands over a serial line and TTL pulse; the stimulator's own safety limits still apply.
  • It is not a robot controller. It sends poses; the robot controller owns the motion, the force limits and the collision checking.
